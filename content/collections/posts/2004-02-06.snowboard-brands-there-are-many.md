<div id="entrytext"><a class="imagelink" href="http://zaskoda.com/wp-content/uploads/2006/09/5.jpg" title="My Friend Randy On His Burton"><img align="right" alt="My Friend Randy On His Burton" id="image13" src="http://zaskoda.com/wp-content/uploads/2006/09/5.thumbnail.jpg"/></a>As I've mentioned before, there are a LOT of snowboard manufacturers, <a href="http://4snowboarding.4anything.com/4/0,1001,6364,00.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="4Snowboarding"> and here are a few</a>. The first name on that list is, without a doubt, the biggest name in snowboards, <a href="http://www.burton.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Burton - Get Your Board">Burton</a>. Burton was there when snowboarding was just getting started and they're well known for high quality gear. I have heard some say that, when you buy Burton, you're paying for a name and can get a comparable board at a lower cost elsewhere. However, everyone agrees that buying a Burton means buying a good board.The best source I found for information on boards thus far is the <a href="http://www.transworldsnowboarding.com/snow/buyers_guide/article/0,13009,475200,00.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Online Version of the 04 Buyers Guide">2004 Snowboard Buyers Guide</a> from the editors of <a href="http://www.transworldsnowboarding.com/snow/magazine/0,14323,,00.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Transworld">TransWorld SNOWBoarding</a>. The various <a href="http://www.transworldsnowboarding.com/snow/buyers_guide/article/0,13009,491251,00.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="online version of top 10 men's boards">top ten lists</a> in this zine include these brands: <a href="http://www.academysnowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Academy Snowboards">Academy</a>, <a href="http://www.arborsports.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Arbor Sports">Arbor</a>, <a href="http://www.atomicsnowboarding.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Atomic Snowboards">Atomic</a>, <a href="http://www.burton.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Burton - Get Your Board">Burton</a>, <a href="http://www.flow.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Flow International (Hope I have the right site)">Flow</a>, <a href="http://www.forum-snowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Forum Snowboards">Forum</a>, <a href="http://www.optionsnowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Option Snowboards">Option</a>, <a href="http://www.ridesnowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Ride Snowboards">Ride</a>, <a href="http://www.romesnowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Rome Snowboard Design Syndicate">Rome</a>, <a href="http://www.salomonsports.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Salomon Sports">Salomon</a>, <a href="http://www.simsnow.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Sims Snowboards">Sims</a>, and <a href="http://www.unitysnowboards.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="Unity Snowboards">Unity</a>.Although I can't really afford it at the moment, I'd really like to get an <a href="http://froogle.google.com/froogle?q=arbor+mystic&amp;btnG=Search+Froogle" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="The Arbor Mystic on Froogle">Arbor Mystic</a>. This board really impresses me. The most notable thing is it's bamboo construction. Its light and strong with lots of flex. The only drawback is the fact it's designed for half-pipes so it has a stiffer tail than I probably need. But that's ok, cause the '04 board has a <a href="http://i.timeinc.net/snow/content/images/03/SNBG04_bt02_M.jpg" rel="noopener" target="_blank" title="image of the Arbor Mystic 04">sexy graphic</a>.</div>

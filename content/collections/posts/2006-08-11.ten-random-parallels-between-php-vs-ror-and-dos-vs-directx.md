<a href="http://zaskoda.blogspot.com/2006/06/ruby-on-rails-thinking-about-test.html">As promised</a>, I started exploring Ruby on Rails... slowly... I installed <a href="http://instantrails.rubyforge.org/wiki/wiki.pl">Instant Rails</a> and it lay dormant for weeks before I started to explore. I think I'm resisting the change. There's something about right now that reminds me of something about ten years ago - and this troubles me.<br/><br/>Coming out of high school in 1994, I had a lot of programming projects in the works. A big one, The Legend of Talibah, was a DOS based console style (mid 90's console) RPG. As I worked on TLOT, I turned to the Internet for support of all kinds. What I found was a computer game development scene up in arms in a debate between DOS based game development and what Win95 promised to bring to the gaming world - DirectX.<br/><br/>Here are 10 random parallels I see between now and then:<br/><table class="contentTable" width="90%"><tbody><tr><th>RoR vs PHP</th><th>DOS vrs DirectX</th></tr><tr><td>Everyone is debating RoR vs PHP.</td><td>Everyone debated DirectX vs DOS.</td></tr><tr><td>Some of the people evangelizing RoR are respected friends.</td><td>Some of the people evangelizing DirectX were respected friends.</td></tr><tr><td>RoR means learning something new.</td><td>DirectX means learning something new.</td></tr><tr><td>RoR abstracts us further from the system.</td><td>DirectX abstracts us further from the system.</td></tr><tr><td>PHP has a big community and lots of libraries while RoR is relatively new and unproven.</td><td>DOS had a big community and lots of libraries while DirectX was relatively new and unproven.</td></tr><tr><td>PHP has lots of frameworks to choose from while Rails just has ROR.</td><td>DOS had lots of frameworks to choose from while Win95 just had DirectX.</td></tr><tr><td>RoR has a huge pile of code before you ever get started building something.</td><td>DirectX has a huge pile of code before you ever get started building something.</td></tr><tr><td>Most webhosts provide PHP.</td><td>Most systems, even the new Win95 systems, had DOS support.</td></tr><tr><td>RoR is being pushed by the Mac camp.</td><td>DirectX was being pushed by the MS camp.</td></tr><tr><td>Every new copy of OSX will have RoR support.</td><td>Every new copy of Windows would have DirectX support.</td></tr></tbody></table><br/><br/>So, I kept working on The Legend of Talibah. Against the advise of many peers, I continued the project in DOS. I had libraries that did everything I wanted from joystick support to audio mixing. It lingered on until the end of 1999, when I officially canned the project. There was no room left in the world for my little DOS game. I was on the wrong side of the DOS vs DirectX debate.<br/><br/>I realize this whole thing is a rather unfair comparison. I'm just say'n that I'm feeling that same vibe again.<br/><br/>Hey, at least I was right about one thing. I've never did like Java.<br/>*evil grin*

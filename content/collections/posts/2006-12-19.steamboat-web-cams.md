<a class="imagelink" href="http://zaskoda.com/2006/12/19/steamboat-web-cams/steamboat-controllable-webcam/" id="p110" rel="attachment" title="Steamboat Controllable Webcam"><img alt="Steamboat Controllable Webcam" id="image110" src="http://zaskoda.com/wp-content/uploads/2006/12/steamboat_controllable_webcam.thumbnail.jpg" style="float: right;"/></a>So, I was really frustrated with the <a href="http://www.steamboat.com/mtnCam.aspx?CategoryId=20">web cams on the Steamboat website</a>. It didn't load well on <a href="http://www.mozilla.com/en-US/firefox/">Firefox</a> and all I wanted was to see some pictures. I tried it out on <a href="http://www.opera.com/">Opera</a> with much better results. That's when I found out what all the extra overhead was about. You can actually manipulate their webcams! Push a little button and you can adjust where the camera is pointed for a limited amount of time. If someone else is driving, you get put into a queue. It just almost works too! The feedback has a lot of latency, so it's really difficult to use. However, the idea of controlling a camera about a thousand miles away is pretty nifty. On the flip side, it sure is annoying when someone ELSE has control of the camera!

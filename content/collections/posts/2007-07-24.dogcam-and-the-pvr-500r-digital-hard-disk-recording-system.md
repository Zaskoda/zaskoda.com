<p><a href="http://www.dogcamsport.co.uk/hard-disk-video-recorder.htm"><img alt="DogCam and the PVR-500r Digital Hard Disk Recording System" src="http://zaskoda.com/wp-content/uploads/2007/07/300rkit.jpg" style="float: right"/></a>Tonight I've been on the hunt for a good camcorder to bring with me to <a href="/blog/pucon-bound-snowboarding-in-chile">Chile next Friday</a>. I've been flip flopping between hard drive cameras and MiniDV. For a while, the Panisonic (like <a href="http://www.afterbang.co.uk/2007/07/panasonic-nv-gs300b-camcorder-review.php">Gavin's</a>) was sounding very tempting. They've got this nifty optical stability system. Still, the idea of a hard drive keeps appealing to me. I've been told that a hard drive based camera runs a higher risk of freezing up than a MiniDV cam. That's where this  next product comes in with a big win.</p>
<p><a href="http://www.dogcamsport.co.uk/">DogCam</a> has a couple of helmet cams that hook into digital recording devices. This means you can carry the recorder in your pocket - near your body heat. The <a href="http://www.dogcamsport.co.uk/hard-disk-video-recorder.htm">PVR-500r</a> reads to have an amazing spec, but I haven't really found much material outside of DogCam's website. They claim some very impressive quality recording options and up to 5 hours worth.</p>
<p>Back to the cam itself for a moment, DogCam provides some <a href="http://www.dogcamsport.co.uk/video.htm">videos</a> on their site. Snowboarding through the trees came out particularly neat, imho. The cam is a bit fish-eyed and there's no zoom option so the camera wouldn't really cover all of the same bases a camcorder would. Still, it's a very tempting option. I would love to hear more from anyone who's used one!</p>

When it begins, you won't know it's happening. It will start with something subtle. Maybe you'll go to the grocery store and find that the shelves are poorly stocked. Maybe you'll notice that a couple of gas stations seem to be out of gas. Then something more drastic will happen. Maybe you'll notice that there's not much traffic on the road. Maybe you'll find yourself looking in the paper for local farms because you can't find your veggies at Central Market anymore. That's right, no fresh veggies from Central Market.

Is the world coming to an end? Why yes, it is, actually. Like the story of the frog in a slowly warming pot of water, you won't really understand that things are changing. But, because of the Internet, chances are word will spread quickly. "Our country's infrastructure is failing!" That is, so long as the network stays online.

When power grids go down, cities can't cool themselves in the summer. When the gas lines go out, cities can't warm themselves in the winter. This is happening in the world <a href="http://www.naturalnews.com/News_000694_Iceland_riots_financial_crisis.html">right now</a>.

When financial system grid lock, every system attached to money is in jeopardy. When financial systems fail, entire countries can be thrown into chaos. This is happening <a href="http://uk.reuters.com/article/rbssEnergyNews/idUKLS42358620090128">right now</a>.

The US is in a more fragile state than at any other time in my own life. Perhaps others have lived long enough to see worse. I'm not sure what to do to be prepared for something extreme. For when "shit goes down" as my friend likes to say.

And that's really just it - systems shut down. Maybe, with the level of communication we share now, we will be able to react quickly when it goes down.

Let's pretend an investor came to me, told me he had 15 million dollars, and he wanted to start something on the Internet that would change everything. This blog post is what I would tell the investor.

The Internet is new. When we began, we had a wide collection of Internet applications we used on a regular basis. We had gopher, ftp, telnet, email, and all kinds of Internet applications. Then HTML and the Web browser really took off. Soon, companies were packaging these applications together in bundels. Then they started integrating them into a single application that was, primarilly, a Web browser.

That's when it got more interesting. Web sites were becoming applications themselves, running on servers. To me, it smells like terminals on a mainframe. Anyway, these applications were fairly clunky until we finally introduced one bit of Javascript technology. A single function call gave rise to the era of AJAX applications - and there was much innovation.

Things got really crazy for a while. Then things started to settle down. Frameworks started to rise in various programming languages and for various purposes. The notion of building a Web application became more a discapline of study rather than exploration. Once brilliantly clever innovations are now just pre-packaged widgets in a drop in javascript library. While cute concepts like Twitter can still cause trending, the development of anything truly revolutionary hasn't happened in a long time. We stuck in a box.

One of our great accomplishments, however, is much like the pattern we observed when various Internet applications started to integrate into a single program. All kinds of webapps have started to interconnect and interact with each other.

Still, we're in a box. We're limited in every direction. Javascript got a lot faster with Chrome, and now everyone is following along. However, Javascript has limitations. HTML was powerful, but developers keep slamming up against limitations while they slowly wait for the W3C to define the solution and THEN for the browser developers to implement that solution and FINALLY for users to adopt the latest browser.

Hold on a second. Lets look at what we've built one more time. We have turned the Web browser into an applications platform and hijacked the concept of document displayal (via HTML) and turned it into our user interface. We build on old technology, maintaining backwards compatibility. On top of this we build a massive infrastructure of Web based applications that attracted millions of people every day. Meanwhile, the developers of these applications work under many arcane limitations inhereted from this dated technology. Simple things, like centering vertically, become complicated challenges.

I say, don't spend your 15 million dollar investment building anything for the Web browser, spend it building software to replace the Web browser. Look at all the things developers are doing that require complex solutions and let them accomplish the same goals easier and with more freedom.

Take a long hard evaluation of the client server model we're using now. Re-engineer from the ground up, but don't throw the baby out with the bathwater. If there are technologies that can help build a better mouse trap, use them. But start, at the very beginning, with "what are people doing with what we have?"

Here's why I know it will work. There are lots of curious types out here on the Internet. There's a generation of new people who never knew the world before we had Web browsers. There are people who would enjoy playing with something new. There are people who will want to explore how to build something, not study how to. Give them a platform that creates powerful applications quickly and effectively, and they will explore it.

Most of all, take your time. Don't blow your $15m in a year trying to get something out there. Setup shop and put in for the long haul. Spread your development over 5 years. Publish all your milestones, engage your community, and even look at open sourcing it. Grow it and evolve it based on how people use it. Consider it a gift to the world, to make their lives easier. Some rash attempt at gimmick software will not work.

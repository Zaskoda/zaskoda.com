One of the bright and shiny points on <a href="http://www.linkedin.com/in/zaskoda">my resume</a> is the time I spent at <a href="http://gearboxsoftware.com/">Gearbox Software</a>. I've always held to a rather strict policy of never bad mouthing a past employer, largely to follow the notion of not burning bridges. I'm about to go against that policy.

In the off chance that you are considering employment at Gearbox and have done a search for experience reports, I recommend that you consider other companies before accepting an offer from Gearbox.

More than any other company I have ever worked for, Gearbox is loaded with internal politics and drama. My departure from the company happened when I refused to work under an individual who had blatantly verbally lied to me in an attempt to protect himself while sacrificing another. I left angry, and that makes me sad.

The company president is known for his background as a magician. He's a professional (literally) at "slight of hand." Know this. Understand this.

While Gearbox boasts some of the best royalty percentages in the industry, very few employees see significant payout. Since the golden years of the Halo PC port, the system has continued to become more broken. As a member of a brain trust dedicated to evaluating the system, I learned a lot about it.

The ultimate case of their broken royalty system became apparent when the company began negotiating deals where they would receive advances on future projects to be paid back out of royalties on current projects. This provided a "slight of hand" filter to redirect royalties into the company (which is privately owned) and out of the pockets of the developers themselves. This arrangement - legal and rather common - managed to flatline a potential royalty payout while I was employed there.

I know some passionate and amazingly talented folks working on Borderlands right now. I not only want the game to succeed, but honestly believe it has the potential be one of the best games headed to the market. I doubt my blog post here could ever have any impact on sales of the game. If it did, I would be terribly sad. I want those devs who have been working so very hard on an awesome game to see returns on their investment.

The culture and people (mostly) at Gearbox are really awesome. The office is cool, the snackroom is top notch, and you generally work on good equipment. There are many positives - many.

However, should you, personally, be considering employment there - weigh your options carefully. Be absolutely clear about your job responsibilities. Do not allow the royalty program to influence your salary requirements. Spend plenty of time interacting with your potential lead. If possible, meet other people working for the same lead. 

Be prepared for political positioning. Hopefully you'll be able to hide in a corner, do your job, and let your lead shield you. Chance are, at some point, you will have to deal with an unreasonable amount of drama. If you can handle yourself well, go for it.

Ultimately, don't base any decisions on the word of the company president. His job is spin, and he does it better than anyone I know.

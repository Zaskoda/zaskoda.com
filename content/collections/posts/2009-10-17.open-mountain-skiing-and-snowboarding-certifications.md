<p>In my last post, I talked about accidents on the mountains. I also talked about recent jailing of several snowboarders for causing fatal accidents. I fear that snowboarders are now going to be the target of ongoing media criticisms. My previous post cited an example of a new media TV broadcast where a snowboarder was cited at fault in a case where there were no witnesses to the accident. Other reports said it could have been either a snowboarder or a skier. That kind of bias is not healthy for the development of the snowboarding industry.</p>
<p>In addition to being a snowboarder, I am also a scuba diver. I have my open water <a href="http://www.naui.com/">NAUI</a> certification. The certification was not difficult to obtain and taught me the basics to diving. Before I took the NAUI class, I already had two dives in Mexico under my belt. I took the class because I wanted to dive in places other than Mexico and most countries require you to have some kind of certification. I thought the class would have little to offer me. I was wrong. Now I realize how important that kind of certification is and how close to danger we'd come during our Mexico dives. For example, no one told me <a href="http://scuba.about.com/od/divemedicine/a/flyinganddiving.htm">not to dive before getting on a flight</a> until the NAUI class.</p>
<p>People are dieing while on vacation because of accidents that are happening on the mountain. I see people do dumb things all the time. I've seen kids hanging out talking on the backside of a jump in the park. The last trip out, I saw a older man with his skis across the entrance to the lift line while he was hanging out and waiting on someone up the hill. I didn't wear wrist guards until *after* my first full day of falling off a snowboard. </p>
<p>I want to rant about all the dumb things I see people do; but it's not their fault that they don't know any better. It's our fault. Every time we see someone on the mountain do something dumb, and we don't help them learn to be safe, we're letting bad things happen. However, who are we to assume we know the right way of doing things on the mountain? Mountains and parks have rules and many states even have laws. For example, by Colorado state law, skiers and snowboarders can not hold the resort liable for accidents.</p>
<p>Before you can drive, you have to get a license. I'm glad because, even though I could learn to drive on my own, I want to make sure that YOU'VE taken the class before you drive anywhere near me. The same goes for skiing and snowboarding. I would be glad if I knew the other people on the mountain had been certified. It would make riding a safer and more pleasant experience for all of us. With the mountains getting ever more crowded each season, this sort of thing may become inevitable.</p>

Every now and again I like to make little <a href="/blog/the-next-big-thing-is-an-appliance">predictions</a>. Surprisingly often, they do indeed <a href="/blog/boxee-and-the-other-half-of-the-problem">manifest</a>. I've made stock recommendations that have doubled the money of those who followed through. So far, no one has ever lost a penny following my recommendations. I was on the cusp of the webmaster boom, the community building boom, and the social media boom. I anticipated a global financial meltdown years before it happened.

I'm not empowered with some special gift to see into the future. Half the time, I can't tell what's going on right in front of me. What makes me unique, as far as I can tell, is just a matter of perspective. I try not to make predictions unless I'm confident. Just like every other human, my predictions are no better than the information I have available to me.

My apparent luck at making predictions hasn't lead me to massive wealth. Ironically, with every stock recommendation I've given, it's faithfully been during a time when I had absolutely no resources to invest myself. Somehow, I think that having vested interest blinds me. If I had the money to invest, I would desire that a stock perform well. That desire, I suspect, would cloud my judgement. So long as I can maintain detachment, I can see a lot more patterns. That's why the context of this post is questionable.

In a nutshell, 2010 will be absolute hell. The actual rate of underemployment in the US is nearing the 20% mark; in fact, it could be even worse than that by now. The flow of money is tight. Our leadership is working hard to loosen the flow. So long as money is moving, it's easier to tap into the stream and get a little for yourself. Unfortunately, money doesn't work like water. Even if we got the rivers flowing again, we're still ultimately doomed. Everyone is looking for a voice of positive reinforcement right now. Everyone wants to hear that everything is going to be ok. Well, it's not - and the more you listen to the comforting voices, the more likely you're going to be caught with your pants down.

Let's talk about that river analogy a little more. Rivers flow because the oceans evaporate and rain falls. Every drop of moisture that we pull from the rivers eventually goes back into the river. If you take a drink, you'll eventually expel that water in one way or another. There is a finite amount of water in a closed system. Sure, the atoms that make up the water may change form, but those same atoms can come back together as water again.

We all know what happens when those who are upstream consume too much water; those downstream die. So long as we can strike a balance, everyone can thrive. Economics is often pitched this way, but our economic system does not work this way. Our system has something added - it's called debt. What debt means is those downstream can still drink water when there's no water to drink, so long as they can give some water back later. There's a reason you don't see this debt based concept in natural systems.

We structured our society around a giant pyramid scheme. There exists more debt in the world than there exists money to repay that debt. As we increase the global supply of money, we increase the amount of debt we can maintain without a meltdown. However, when we introduce more money, we devalue all of the existing money in the system - inflation. This system is big, complex, and the patterns span over large amounts of time. You have to take on the right perspective to see it.

We are so attached to the concept of money, that most people can not imagine how the world could possibly function without it. Every other living creature on Earth seems to do just fine without money. However, we humans believe we have to have money to cohabitate on the earth. Maybe we do. Maybe money makes living in large compact spaces (cities) possible. If so, then this type of living must be as unstable and temporary as our economic system itself is.

The United States saw an economic <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression">meltdown of massive proportions</a> just 80 years ago. It's just long enough for most of those who experienced it to have died away. As you read that Wikipedia entry I just linked to, you can dig through complex explanations along with global cause and effect. As for the explanation, it doesn't need to be so complex. In our current economic crisis, just like every single one before, the cause is greed.

Just like a parasite that consumes too much if it's host, greed is self defeating in the long run. The problems is that people view our economic system in selfish terms. Greed can work for "me" for as long as I live. Ultimate, when greed does it's damage, it's generally someone else who suffers. This someone else may very well be an entirely different generation. The suffering that began this year is the result of a great many greedy people working together to run the river dry.

The economy swings between greed and fear. The government is working hard to get the economy moving again, to shift attitudes from fear back to greed. Right now, the greedy are fearful. Those that helped drain the river are holding on to what water they've collected. Because this water is all in storage, the rains will never come. The government "created" more water (more money) to make it look like the river is still flowing. The hope is that those with stores of water will feel safe to let the water flow again. So far, this isn't happening.

Economic systems like ours have been "working" for thousands of years. When one does collapse, we just build another one. The process involves a lot of pain and suffering, but it's what we do. Just because we're setup for massive failure doesn't mean it's going to happen next year. However, as far as I can tell, we're on course for a painful 2010. I believe strongly that things are going to get much works before they get better.

Our current situation is unique to every economic crisis of the past. There are more humans on the Earth than ever before. These humans are linked together in ways that never before existed. If the New York stock exchange crashes Monday morning, we'll know about it in realtime all around the globe. Reactions happen faster now, and with more force than ever before. We have a huge co-dependent ecosystem of economic systems. What we have now is more than a US economic crash. We are seeing the first tremors of a global economic meltdown.

One way or another, we will fix it. The unknown is "how" we will fix it and how long it will take. I like to dream of finding a solution other than money. I like to believe that new communication technologies built from the Internet will somehow bring about a solution no one every dreamed was possible. I also like to believe that I will one day walk on Mars.

I don't know how to prepare for what is about to happen. As far as I can tell, gathering money will soften the blow. However, with the inevitable massive inflation that is coming, cash savings will devalue quickly. That is probably why gold is at an all time high and continues to rise quickly every single day. The temptation for me now is to get into debt. I have good credit and the river still flows enough that I can get a decent loan. The artificially low interest rates put in place to try to stimulate the economy makes this option seem even more viable. If I can borrow money and buy something that will be highly valuable later, I win. But what is that something? And is this not the same greed related action that I was complaining about just a few paragraphs above? And when all the jobs are gone, and I can't pay back the loan, I'm just going to lose my investment to whoever I borrowed the money from.

The only thing that seems to make sense is survival measures. Secure food. Secure land that can sustain life. Secure basic supplies such as tools and fuel. I'm not alone on this. From left wingers to right wingers, more and more people are preparing for bad times. For some, it's simply because they're trying to afford living in the hear and now. A well maintained garden can save a fortune in grocery bills. For others, it seems they can feel that something is coming. I have friends that have altered their entire life courses. People are moving to farms, starting intentional communities and forming living co-ops.

However, we've had survivalists and doomsday sayers for as long as we've been recording history. The truth is, no matter how many times we make good guesses about stock prices or technology changes, we can not know the future. The large scale patterns are easy to identify, but the billions of micro influences that trigger upcoming events are so complex and subtle that no human perception can track them all. We make guesses, the best guesses we can muster.

I have friends who disagree with me entirely. I know many people who feel we will recover from this economic downturn and that we're headed in a more positive global direction than ever before. Of course, I also have friends who are buying guns and friends who are leaving the country altogether. These are my predictions and expectations. In my mind, I'm seeing entire cities without electricity. In my mind, I'm seeing desperate people fighting for limited resources. In my mind, we still have too many greedy people in the global population to find a peaceful balance.

The American dream works because of our natural greed. We grew up and grew up fast because we believed in individual freedom. Unfortunately, we did so at the cost of our collective security. We've grown so complex that current attempts to secure the collective have become nearly impossible - as we see with the current health care debate.

With a little bit of luck, the United States of America will use this opportunity to redefine itself in a way no one has yet dreamed of. We have the potential now, the rare potential, to evolve into something beautiful and amazing. To realize this dream, we must all share the vision. To see the vision of our dream, we must wake from our slumber. To wake from our slumber, we must be lulled from our misconception about what life truly is. Sometimes, waking up can be a bit disturbing. Here's hoping we all wake up real soon.

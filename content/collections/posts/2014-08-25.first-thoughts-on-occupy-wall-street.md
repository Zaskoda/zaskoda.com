We're nearing the end of 2011. Back in 2008, a particularly large economic bubble burst -<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_housing_bubble"> the housing market bubble</a>. Thus precipitated our shift into economic decline. As the average quality of life has slowly started to fall across the US, citizens have grown increasingly angry. Citizens have begun looking for someone to blame. It seems very likely, after 3 years of economic decline, that we are no where near the bottom yet.

In 2009, television media<a href="http://mediamatters.org/reports/200904080025"> supported</a> a series of protests called the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement">Tea Party movement</a>. This movement was fueled by the increasing frustrations of American citizens. However, this movement was guided by a political agenda. Major media outlets are paid through advertising networks and are heavily influenced by political interests. The Tea Party movement quickly became a Republican backed campaign and often seemed to do little more than attack the recently elected Democratic presidency.

Ignoring all concepts politics, the economy itself simply continued to decline. A friend of mine described it as if Obama was going to take the failing American economy and bring it in for a survivable crash landing. This seems to be what is happening. We've reached the end of peak oil. Our economy is no long grounded in the production of goods and our currency is no longer backed by gold. We're in free fall.

The Internet is our first many to many communications medium in the history of human kind. For the first time, ever, any human with access to the system can broadcast a message with the potential to be read by a significantly large part of the overall population. With this tool, we've started developing tools to work as a collective. We use these tools to share media, archive information, and broadcast news. The technology itself is rudimentary, but it's working. A new generation exists that never knew a world without the Internet.

Today, there are hundreds of members of this new generation involved in a protest on the epicenter of our financial structure - <a href="http://occupywallst.org/">Wall Street</a>. These protesters represent a distinctly different demographic from their Tea Party predecessors. While big media supported the Tea Party movement, perhaps even created it, they have no vested interest in the Occupy Wall Street movement. Instead, this movement is being backed by groups such as the hacker collective known as <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_(group)">anonymous</a>.

I believe that some day the hacker will be known as the ultimate freedom fighter. Not just groups like anonymous, but every phreak that has refused to obey superficial rules in a virtual world we should all equally own. It is this resistant attitude that has held back the rapid and aggressive grabs at control over the Internet. The <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_neutrality">net neutrality</a> effort is attempting to keep ISP from gaining control over what you can and can't access. The free culture movement is working to keep IP laws from overtaking online culture. The <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate_Parties_International">pirate party</a> is another example of digital culture's backlash against IP law.

In recent years, our new Internet culture's lovechild, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks">Wikileaks</a>, tore down the idea that the public would never see government secrets. Julian Assange exposed top secret government documents to the world. He showed the world video footage of American weapons slaying innocent people. He was only able to do this because of this new many to many communications platform called the Internet. He did this on a free piece of software designed to support collaborative writing and archiving projects.

With their smart phones, tablets, netbooks, and laptops, the protesters currently occupying the financial district of New York City do not represent one key interest. For this, some traditional news media outlets <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvhQUR4LdXo&amp;feature=player_embedded">are belittling them</a>. That's because traditional media assumes that there is one voice behind any group of people. Traditional media assumes someone somewhere has control.

The occupy wall street movement and the energy that helped spawn it are more akin to a game of Katamari Damacy. Most recently, airline pilots and labor unions have shown up on Wall Street to join in on some protesting. The intention of the effort is to occupy Wall Street for months. Despite questionable arrests of hundreds of protesters, the movement is still going after 19 days. We'll see if it keeps growing or falls to pieces.

There is an inherent misunderstanding about these protests. As people become increasing angry and looking for someone to blame, we citizens look to the politicians and the bankers. We look to the rich, assuming that since they have all the money they must somehow be in control of the system. We look 'up' the food chain and scream "WTF?" Then we wonder why no one speaks back.

Our problem represents generation after generation of gradual corruption and degradation. The greedy bankers who nearly destroyed the world's economy are rich. They were rewarded for their behavior. We might like to believe we're better than they are. However, given the same opportunities and rewards, many of us would have made the same decisions. Many of us would have become the same exact types of people. Only, we're the people who lost in this system. We're the people who feel powerless.

The problem is the system we designed that allowed money to take over the people's voice. The ideas that drive us, such as the idea that money has value, are the ideas controlling us. The system of teaching, from schools to mass media, were only able to bring us so far. But with the dawn of a new ear, a many to many communications tool, we are re-evaluating everything. As the world we built before collapses under our feet, we are looking to this new way to guide us.

Now, as our leadership fails us, we will do what every generation who faced this time before us did. We will turn to each other and form communities to support each other. We will struggle, we will fight, and we will very likely survive. However, this time we have a piece of technology that allows for a sense of one single global community. Perhaps, if we can keep this technology in the hands of the many, we will one day have only us and no them.

The children of the Internet are on Wall Street. The collective is speaking. There are no one liners or cheat sheets, to listen, you have to jack in.

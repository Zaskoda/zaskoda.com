<h3><strong>Introduction</strong></h3>
This hope this is one of the most important blog posts I've composed. Perhaps it will see as much exposure as my #1 hit post on the blog, <a href="http://zaskoda.com/2009/05/20/kitchen-sponge-best-practices/">Kitchen Sponge Best Practices</a>.

I have something to say to the folks involved with Web stuff at Burning Man Org, particularly those working on "Profiles". Before I begin, I would like to disclose that, while I am currently an <a href="http://apogaea.com/apo-org/ignition/scott-koda-dudley/">active Apogaea board member</a>, my opinion here does not reflect the opinion of <a href="http://apogaea.com/apo-org/">Apogaea Inc</a> or anyone involved with Apogaea. Regardless, everything I have to say is entirely correct.

Profiles is a logical next step in solving logistical problems. Profiles is more than that. Profiles is an opportunity to change the world in a very positive and a very powerful way.

<h3><strong>The Background</strong></h3>
The <a href="http://blog.burningman.com/2013/01/news/announcing-burner-profiles/">Burning Man blog</a> recently announced <a href="http://profiles.burningman.com/">Burner Profiles</a>. They gave us this line,
<blockquote><strong>"Eventually we’ll be expanding the use of this new platform to offer additional features."</strong></blockquote>
A couple of months earlier, on October 26th, I wrote the Apogaea board of directors the following:
<blockquote>Hi Mission Control,
<div></div>
<div>I wanted to update you on my intended software/web project for this year. The returning board members might remember Cano mentioning this project before he retired. I'm going to detail the approach a little more.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Nutshell:<strong> The end result will look like a digital org chart where Ignition members can log in and update their own profiles</strong> (to a degree).</div>
<div></div>
<div>What's really happening: I'm going to build a registration database with the intention of ultimately opening it up to the entire community. Think of it like Facebook where all you can do is sign up and create a "profile" - nothing else. The "profile" will be used in the org chart very much the way the current Ignition page works, only with much better graphic representation of the structure of the org. For reference, here's the current page:</div>
<div></div>
<div><a href="http://apogaea.com/apo-org/ignition/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">http://apogaea.com/apo-org/<wbr/>ignition/</a></div>
<div></div>
<div>What will be different:</div>
<div> - Ignition members will be able to log in, update their bio, and update their photo.</div>
<div> - Expired Ignition members won't be deleted, they'll just fall out into the "community" category and retain their history of service information in their profile.</div>
<div> - Community members can sign up and create a profile, they just won't appear on the org chart.</div>
<div> - Org chart view will be more detailed and interactive, better showing the structure of the org.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Building an org chart is a reasonably achievable goal. By taking this approach, we also create a platform on which we can build more tools. For example, if Bug is up for it, we can use this user database in the volunteer database tool and track the history of volunteerism per individual. Other ideas include: a voting tool, an idea submission tool, a forum, tools for early access ticket purchases, etc.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Currently I am evaluating technology stacks for the system. I'm trying to find the right balance between keeping true to our current stack (linux, php, mysql, apache, wordpress) and exploring the introduction of new tech in the stack (another DB such as Mongo, an MVC framework such as cakePHP or Kohana, and perhaps a different scripting language such as Python or Ruby). Once I pick the stack, the development cycle shouldn't take me that long. It's not a complex project.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Note: the design of this software may be impacted by our conversations about the overall structure of Apogaea including not only Ignition, but the greater community of volunteers and attendees.</div>
<div></div>
<div>~Koda</div></blockquote>
So I intended to donate some time building an online profile system for Apogaea. I have yet to start; however, I felt weird when I read the Burner Profiles announcement. I really believe that many great ideas are simply intuitive and  often happen spontaneously in many places. For both Burning Man and Apogaea, basic online profiles offer intuitive solutions.

I would like to further provide some evidence that I've been pondering this solution for some time. The following was composed on February 14th, 2012 (about a year ago). I sent it to <a href="http://www.linkedin.com/in/katsteinmetz">Kat Steinmetz</a> and <a href="http://www.cameragirl.com/">Heather "Camera Girl" Phelps-Lipton</a> at <a href="http://www.burningman.com/whatisburningman/organization/exec_committee.html">Burning Man Org</a>. This was a follow up to a previous dialog where I had applied and interviewed for their open positions of Web Producer. Hopefully they won't mind me sharing it as I wrote it to begin with:
<blockquote>Kat, Heather,
<div></div>
<div>Since our last exchange I was voted into the Apogaea (Colorado's regional event) Board of Directors. One of my roles is communications lead. We've started a process of reorganizing our communications infrastructure. One of our core components, our website, was a blocker to effectively communicating with our community. We're very proud to have just launched a redesign of our Web infrastructure. We didn't just change the look, but reworked our entire information architecture. In the process, we also moved everything to a new hosting provider, Dreamhost (free for 501(3)(c) nonprofits). The site represents some 300+ hours of volunteer time. We're still ironing out bugs (mostly rendering on older browsers), but we've made significant improvements. I am itching to share these with the Burning Man goddesses.</div>
<div></div>
<div><a href="http://apogaea.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">http://Apogaea.com</a></div>
<div></div>
<div>Of course, my pride is not so distracting that I wouldn't welcome constructive feedback.  So if you're inclined to pick it apart, dig in and have fun.</div>
<div></div>
<div>We've done more than a website redesign to upgrade our communications infrastructure. In the past, Apogaea has communicated through generalized "Burning Man" channels. Although an Apogaea Yahoo! mailing list group was eventually established, the org was still broadcasting and engaging across nearly a dozen different hot spots of Burner activity. We don't really need to advertise. Our event sold out rather early last year. We've used this momentum to pull our communications in tighter. We've significantly reduced the volume of information hitting the generalized burner hot spots and are focusing on driving traffic to Apo specific channels. Since I came on last summer, our Facebook group has gone from around 800 members to about 1500. The Colorado Burning Man Facebook group is less than half that size. Given that last year's event was capped at 1100 attendees, I feel pretty good about the size and activity of our Facebook group.</div>
<div></div>
<div><a href="/assets/blog/2013/01/volunteer_db.png"><img alt="" class="alignright size-thumbnail wp-image-5754" height="150" src="/assets/blog/2013/01/volunteer_db-150x150.png" title="Snapshot of Apogaea Volunteer Database" width="150"/></a>The momentum right now is to move to self-hosted and potentially self-written communications tools. We'll likely move away from the Yahoo! lists in favor of our own <a href="http://apogaea.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">apogaea.com</a> branded list serves. We may also start running our own forum. <strong>More likely, we're discussing the design and development of a custom community platform that is more closely designed for our type of community. For example, our community is full of makers, artists, and performers. They are constantly holding events like workshops, performances, and art fairs. We've been discussing tools to specifically support this. We are also discussing open sourcing these tools and providing guidance and training to other regionals that might want to adopt our platform.</strong> This was a learn-by-example process as we were evaluating the open source Block Rock Ticket System (I'm guessing you are familiar).<strong> We may look into writing our own ticketing system next year. We currently have a custom volunteer signup system and the notion of having all of our needs rolled into one coherent solution is exciting.</strong> We've already started maintaining our systems in version control software. I'm looking to grow a local developers network sometime after this year's event so we will be better equipped to take on these tasks.</div>
<div></div>
<div>We're all watching the Burning Man ticket system and anticipating STEP. As our event has begun to sell out, we're discussing ticketing solutions now. Hope you guys aren't fighting too many fires on that end, I'm sure there's plenty of work to do. One of my passions in life is understanding how simple choices in systems or software can have huge rippling impacts on a community as a whole. I guess that's why I've been doing this stuff for more than a decade and still can't get enough... now I'm doing it for free of all things. ;)</div>
<div></div>
<div>If either of you would recommend any well-organized regionals, perhaps working on the same problems as us or better yet, already having solutions, please let me know.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Sat Nam,</div>
<div>Koda</div></blockquote>
<div>I didn't hear back. Anyway, the key point is in bold above.</div>
<div></div>
<div>From all I have written thus far, you might think I am suggestion that Burning Man org created Burner Profiles based on what I said. No, Burner Profiles was an obvious next step to solve an ongoing paradigm. What I am suggesting is that I've been pondering this "software" possibility in detail for months. What I have to express in this open letter isn't a momentary impulse, but a culmination of years of passionate interest. The truth is, I had hoped the Web Producer position at Burning Man would afford me the opportunity to be master minding their solution. Shot down, I took that energy into my local regional, Apogaea. The passion has changed, but it never died. Now that it seems Burning Man org is heading down this path, I have a few things to say.</div>
<div></div>
<div>Burning Man and The Internet</div>

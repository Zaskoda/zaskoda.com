What would you need in your life to give up money? It's a simple question. The answer may be considerably more complex.

I grew up knowing that I was going to be rich. I knew I had the right stuff. I'm 33 years in, still not living the life of fortune. After 33 years, I've also mostly disconnected from the desire to be rich. I noticed a pattern and then read about it later. It's a simple pattern. The pattern starts when you find yourself dreaming about doing things that require a bit more time and money than you have. Next, something changes in your life where you suddenly have more time and money. Finally, you find that once you do have the time and money, you don't do that thing you kept dreaming about. Curious pattern this is.

I'm eager to see <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JeROnVUADj0">Capitalism by Michael Moore</a>. I haven't yet. I've been thinking about a number of things I want to blog about, but am holding back until I have. Still, I can't stop thinking about this one thing - our attachment to the idea of money.

It's seemingly clearer and clearer to me lately that people really love money. Nino and I talked about how neither of us have the cut throat attitude it takes to step on other people in the goal to collect wealth. In reflection, I'm pretty sure that's a big part of why I've never been particularly wealthy. I recently did a project that required sub contractors. I got stepped on (iow, I didn't get paid). Yet, I'm still honoring my debt to my sub contractors. So the subs get paid for their work and the client got a ton of work for free. Meanwhile, I'm out thousands. Yeah, you can't get rich that way.

But there's another big piece here I don't think people understand. If our system were a perfect <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-sum">zero sum game</a>, it would be impossible for someone to collect wealth without taking wealth from someone else. Our system is actually worse - because it includes the idea of interest. Because loaned money creates "interest" and all money enters the system via debt, there's actually more money owed than there exists in the system. This only makes the paradigm I just mentioned that much more intense. For someone to go 'up', someone else has to go down.

We've done a great job of masking this in the US. We started with slaves. Eventually, we reached a point where we just exploit other nation's cheap labor. It's so complicated and abstract, most people don't notice it.

But at the end of the day, every dollar you 'earn' is a dollar someone else can't have... So you can't have rich w/out having poor.

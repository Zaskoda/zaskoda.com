For a couple of years, I've been running <a href="https://www.google.com/adsense/">Google Adsense</a> ads on several projects. I must admit, I was really excited about the idea when they came out... See, I've always <a href="http://www.marketingterms.com/dictionary/banner_ad/">hated ads</a>. Unfortunately, some of my job functions have related to creating and placing ad banners in places and ways that I feel are too intrusive. Google ads proved that ads can be effective yet unintrusive. Very cool.

Google ads are not only designed to be unintrusive, but come with a <a href="http://ezinearticles.com/?How-To-Prevent-Your-Adsense-Account-Getting-Terminated&amp;id=140601">lot of restrictions</a> for use. In a nutshell, all their restrictions are efforts to ensure that all "clicks" are legit. From an advertisers point of view, I'm sure this is very much appreciated. Even from a content provider's point of view, I appreciate the enforcement of such rules. Google Adsense uses a <a href="http://www.myusefullinks.co.uk/various/Googleadsense15.htm">dynamic system</a> for determining the value of a click. Folks using bots or even manual methods of running up clicks on their ads dilute the system, ultimately devaluing my clicks - the dirty rats.

This makes me wonder, how can I <a href="https://www.google.com/support/adsense/bin/static.py?page=tips.html">make the ads as effective as possible</a>? In the past, I've made sure that the ads don't distract from the content on the page. By doing so, I decrease their effectiveness and ultimately cost myself money. It could be argued that the higher quality of a cleaner site will garnish more traffic and ultimately more potential clicks... But in the real world, <a href="http://www.seroundtable.com/archives/003519.html">ugly web sites do just fine</a>.

Suggestions included placing the ads right in the middle of content zones on the website. This, to me, seemed to go against the ethic that the system was pushig to begin with. However, I don't make the rules - I just follow them.

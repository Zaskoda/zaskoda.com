With all the buzz about <a href="http://bubble20.blogspot.com/">a new tech bubble</a> combined with the every changing <a href="http://socialsoftware.weblogsinc.com/entry/9817137581524458/">face of the Web</a> and a whole new approach to how <a href="http://www.costik.com/weblog/2005_09_01_blogchive.html#112751023848334701">we think about business</a> online - I would dare to say that we are in a bit of a cultural revolution, complete with annoying <a href="http://web2.wsj2.com/web2ishere.htm">b</a><a href="http://socialsoftware.weblogsinc.com/entry/1234000170061010/">u</a><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0">z</a><a href="http://www.web2con.com/">z</a><a href="http://www.digital-web.com/articles/web_2_for_designers/">w</a><a href="http://www.uie.com/events/uiconf/articles/web_2_power/">o</a><a href="http://www.25hoursaday.com/weblog/PermaLink.aspx?guid=e481327e-5e8b-4b93-982e-db206222a2cf">r</a><a href="http://radar.oreilly.com/archives/2005/08/not_20.html">d</a><a href="http://www.mattmckeon.com/fh/?p=235">s</a>.

Annoying or not, the change seems good. Smart people are coming up with good ideas; more importantly, they're making good ideas happen. As I've mentioned <a href="http://zaskoda.blogspot.com/2005/05/virtual-community-what-is-it-were.html">on this website</a> and <a href="http://www.sitepoint.com/forums/showpost.php?p=2137668&amp;postcount=4">elsewhere</a>, I believe that one of the most significant changes we are going to see over the next few years deals with the way we think about virtual communities.

Forums Are Bad

<span style="font-weight: bold;">Space, what is it?</span>

It's all around you. <a href="http://dictionary.reference.com/search?q=space">S</a><a href="http://www.google.com/search?q=define%3A+space">p</a><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space">a</a><a href="http://www.wordreference.com/definition/space">c</a><a href="http://thesaurus.reference.com/search?q=space">e</a> is infinite, but also microscopic. We humans understand the space in which we exist as three dimensional and spread over a 4th dimension we understand as time. All that we experience is contained within the context of space and time. But how does space relate to virtual communities?

<span style="font-weight: bold;">How does space affect culture and community?</span>

Space is, perhaps, the most significant cultural influence that can be identified. Before a modern world of communication existed, space seperated groups of people. This division between groups of people allowed for the development of different and unique cultural identities.

Our generation had the honor of watching world culture begin to collide due to the growth of communications technology and eventually the many-to-many medium we call the Internet. Now, subcultures can be born strictly online with complete disregard to anyone's location in space. If only we could overcome time the same way.

<span style="font-weight: bold;">What does this have to do with virtual communities?</span>

Many platforms for developing virtual communities strive for ease of use. Generally, exploring forums and chat rooms is relatively easy. Not only can you quickly jump from one forum or room to another quickly, but designers actually make an effort to provide the quickest and easiest interfaces possible to move from one conceptual area of content to the next.

There's nothing wrong with this approach. In fact, in most cases it works very well. However, we humans spent thousands of years developing our brains to understand a spacial world. Therefore, why not build our interfaces to take advantage of this concept?

There are cases in which this model of virtual community is extremely successful, such as the world of <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mmorpg">MMORPG</a>s. While playing World of Warcraft, one does not expect to suddenly jump into a conversation halfway across the game world simply by clicking a single link.

<span style="font-weight: bold;">What does space provide and how can we use it?</span>

For a community to bond, the members of that community must have a trait that connects them. For an online poker club, the common bond is the love of poker. A common bond allows community members to maintain a rapport with one another. Often times, online communities fail because the common bond among community members is too weak or abstract.

Space provides a common bond. Your neighbors have something in common with you, they inhabit the same general area of the earth as you. Hense, you're are more likely to be familiar with their lives than a random individual living in another country.

As Christopher Allen so clearly illustrated, <a href="http://www.lifewithalacrity.com/2005/03/dunbar_altruist.html">communities follow grouping behaviors</a>. As a community reaches a significantly large size, it will become less stable. To keep the community stable, there must be a method of developing sub communities (i.e. having multiple chat rooms instead of one single room). However, to be a strong community, these sub communities must have their own common bonds.

The obvious point is, why not use the natural concept of space to facilitate a common bond within these sub communities?

<span style="font-weight: bold;">Wait, what's the point here?</span>

The concept of space is a powerful tool. How perceived space affects the development of a community is an interesting puzzle.

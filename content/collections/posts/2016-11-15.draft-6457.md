I studied at the ATLAS Institute, part of CU Boulder. I earned an MS in a multidisciplinary program called <a href="http://atlas.colorado.edu/programs/ictd-track/">Information and Communications Technology for Development</a>. Academically, it was an intersection of technology, business, and social science. In part of the program, we studied folks like Jeffrey Sachs and Tim Unwin on issues of global poverty crisis; and we explored the proper methods for doing global development fieldwork. I learned a lot of neat stuff, but I'm not hear to talk about any of that interesting stuff. Rather, this is a rant about politics and I needed a setup. And that setup begins with a story.

Before I tell you the story, I'd like to give you a better understanding of global development work. Some development work goes well. The <a href="http://www.newyorker.com/tech/elements/could-harvesting-fog-help-solve-the-worlds-water-crisis">fog nets in Moracco</a> were a total win. The nets collected fog and made clean drinking water out of - thin air. Still, getting the recipients of that water to use it responsibly was another story altogether. Still, as far as most development work goes, the project was a smashing success. That is, compared to projects like <a href="http://blogs.ei.columbia.edu/2010/07/01/the-playpump-what-went-wrong/">the play pump</a>. Unfortunately, for most of the well-intentioned development projects, failure is the most likely result. At least those who were in my program were able to benefit from the education those failures provide. It probably won't stop us from failing, but maybe we can look forward to failing in some new and creative way. The truth is, development work is hard and it's easy to overlook something that turns out to be obvious in retrospect.

So my story begins: one day in class, I was talking to other students about the very popular subject of elevating women in cultures where they are oppressed. We talked about a specific example where the women lacked access to banking services and were largely excluded from ownership. The program we discussed would use a micro-finance model to empower women. As we rolled along and talked, we all basically flowed and agreed. The goal was to empower the women to do something the men used to do. So I asked, "so what about the men?" My question was met with silence. I continued, "We're proposing to change the lives of these women by empowering them to take on the roles that used to be exclusive to men. The men will no longer have a purpose. Their role in the community will need to change. What do we do to help the men through that transition?" I got but one reply before we were interrupted by the professor to move on in our activity, "I never thought of that."

To some degree, I get it. The focus is on elevating women. Global Millennial Goal number 3 is: Promote gender equality and empower women. However, to be effective with our goals we have to consider the breadth of the complex problem. It's easy to dismiss the men in the community because they are perceived as being in a position of power. However, they are connected members of their community and need to be considered in the larger scope of development if for no more reason than failing to do so will lead to your program failing in general.

And we're finally to the part where I promised to rant about politics. The 2016 election for President is over and Donald Trump is our President Elect. The Democratic Party not only lost the Presidency, but is a minority in both houses and have lost many state legislatures as well. A combination of globalization thanks to American trade deals made in the last couple of decades along with the rapid rise of automation lead to the deterioration of our manufacturing base. The focus on renewable fuels lead to massive job loss in the fossil fuel industry, especially coal. When Democrats made well intentioned plans to fight climate change and connect the world's economies, they failed to take into account the workers who would be displaced. There was no plan to create jobs. Someone hadn't thought about that.

Two candidates during this election cycle knew how to speak to the people in our country who were otherwise not being heard. The Democrats turned away one of them, Bernie Sanders. The Republicans fought but were unable to stop Donald Trump.
